Bitwise Signed Right Shift Operator >>

The signed right shift operator shifts all the bits to the right.

The empty positions created on the left side are filled depending on the sign of the input number:

If the number is negative, its Most Significant Bit (MSB) is 1, so the empty positions are filled with 1s.
If the number is positive, its MSB is 0, so the empty positions are filled with 0s.

This is called sign extension because the sign bit is extended into the newly created positions.

For example:

Positive:

00010100  >> 2

00000101
^^^^
filled with 0



Negative:

11101100  >> 2

11111011
^^^^
filled with 1

So the key point to emphasize to students is:

>> preserves the sign by filling the left side with copies of the sign bit.

That is precisely why Java calls >> the signed right shift operator.
